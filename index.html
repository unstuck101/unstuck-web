Before You Ask

Frequently Asked Questions

◆
Is the cash flow really higher than traditional long-term rentals?

Typically, yes. Sober living operators sign multi-year leases — often at above-market rents — because the home is their business. You get commercial-grade lease terms on a residential asset, which is where the stronger cash-on-cash returns come from.

What about the neighbors of these homes?

Well-run sober living homes are structured, drug- and alcohol-free environments with house rules, house managers, and accountability. In practice they're often quieter and better maintained than the average rental — and the operators we partner with are vetted on exactly this.

How is the investment passive if I own the entire asset?

We identify the property, negotiate the purchase, get the operator lease signed before closing, and manage any rehab. After closing you can self-manage or use a local property management company — most owners simply collect rent from a single long-term tenant.

I already own a rental — can you set it up as a sober living home?

Often, yes. The bones, layout, and location matter, and not every home qualifies — but conversions are part of what we do. Join the buyer's list and mention your property on the intro call.

What happens if the operator ever leaves?

Your downside is protected by the asset itself: you own a standard single-family home in a normal neighborhood. We'll help you re-lease it to another vetted operator — or it simply rents as a conventional property.
